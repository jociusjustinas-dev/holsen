Truck Owners

Introduce your equipment, operating routes and available transport capacity.

Transport cooperation

Capacity matched to a defined route.

Why we connect
International road operations need capacity that matches the route, timing, cargo and equipment requirements.
What we look for
Truck owners and carriers who can clearly define their equipment, lanes, availability, restrictions and qualification scope.
How cooperation works
Holsen reviews the fit for a specific requirement, clarifies the required records and agrees scope and commercial terms before work begins.

What to include

Equipment & capacity
Vehicle and trailer types, payload and loading dimensions; available capacity and timing.
Routes & availability
Countries and routes you operate, preferred lanes and recurring or occasional availability.
Operating scope
Cargo restrictions, handling capabilities and the licences and insurance relevant to your proposal.

Warehousing

Outline your facility, storage capacity and the handling services you can provide.

Warehousing cooperation

A facility matched to the handling need.

Why we connect
Some transport plans need storage, cross-docking or local handling close to a collection, transfer or delivery point.
What we look for
Warehousing providers who can define their location, available space, access, equipment, operating hours and cargo restrictions.
How cooperation works
The facility and handling scope are reviewed for a specific requirement before responsibilities, access arrangements and commercial terms are agreed.

What to include

Location & space
Facility location, available storage capacity, access arrangements and operating hours.
Handling & services
Loading equipment, storage conditions, cross-docking and any additional handling you provide.
Scope & restrictions
Accepted cargo types, operational limits and any relevant insurance or certification scope.

03

Local Distribution

Define your delivery area, vehicle setup and collection and delivery arrangements.

Distribution cooperation

The final leg, clearly coordinated.

Why we connect
International movements can need a regional collection or delivery leg shaped by local access, vehicle and delivery-window requirements.
What we look for
Distribution partners with defined coverage, suitable vehicles, reliable dispatch contacts and a clear delivery-confirmation process.
How cooperation works
The delivery area, handover points, update process, responsibilities and commercial terms are agreed for the specific operation.

What to include

Delivery area
Cities or regions covered, collection points and any geographic or site-access restrictions.
Delivery setup
Vehicle types, shipment sizes, unloading equipment and delivery-window options.
Coordination
Dispatch contacts, shipment updates, delivery confirmation and arrangements for exceptions.

Which types of partner can introduce their services?

This page covers truck owners, warehousing and local distribution partners. Describe the services you provide, the area you cover and your available capacity.

Explore the three partner categories

What should a first introduction include?

Start with your company name, operating location and business contact. Add your equipment or facilities, coverage, available capacity and relevant operating restrictions. The checklist above sets out what is useful for each type of partner.

Review the introduction checklist

Are shipment volumes or payment terms confirmed here?

No. This page does not offer guaranteed volumes or set payment terms. Scope, availability, responsibilities and commercial conditions need to be agreed for the proposed cooperation.

Should I include specialist capabilities?

Yes. Describe the exact scope, relevant equipment, restrictions and supporting qualifications. A general capability description is not confirmation that a specific cargo or operation can be accepted.
